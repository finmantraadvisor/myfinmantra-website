Free cash flow statement format in Excel — indirect method

Prepare a cash flow statement in minutes from two balance sheets and a handful of profit and loss figures. Built-in checks tell you if anything doesn't tie.

What's included

  • Indirect method in the AS 3 / Ind AS 7 format
  • Working capital changes worked out from the two balance sheets
  • Purchase of fixed assets derived from the movement in net PPE, depreciation and assets sold
  • Operating, investing and financing activities with sub-totals
  • Check that computed closing cash agrees with the balance sheet
  • Reserves check to catch missing items

How to use it

  1. Enter previous and current year balance sheets on the Balance Sheet tab; the check row must show zero.
  2. Enter profit before tax, depreciation, finance costs, tax paid and the other items on the P&L & Other Info tab.
  3. Open the Cash Flow Statement tab — it is ready, with a tick when it ties to the balance sheet.

Blue cells on a yellow background are inputs; everything else is a live formula, so totals and results update as you type. Sample figures are filled in so you can see how it works.

Questions

What is the indirect method?

It starts with profit before tax, adds back non-cash items such as depreciation, removes non-operating items such as interest income, and adjusts for changes in working capital to arrive at cash from operations.

Why doesn't my cash flow tie?

Usually because a balance sheet movement isn't explained by profit, dividends or the inputs provided — for example, a revaluation, bonus issue or securities premium. Check the reserves difference on the P&L & Other Info tab.

Can I use it for Ind AS companies?

Yes, the layout follows Ind AS 7 and AS 3. Classification choices for interest and dividends should follow your company's accounting policy.
