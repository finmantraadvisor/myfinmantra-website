Income-tax Act 2025: what changes for you from Tax Year 2026-27

The new Income-tax Act replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026. Here's what actually changed — and what stayed the same.

By Ankit · 23 October 2026 · 4 min read

From 1 April 2026, income tax in India is governed by the Income-tax Act, 2025, which replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961. The good news for most taxpayers: it is mainly a simplification of language and structure. Tax rates and slabs did not change, and Budget 2026 made no changes to the slabs either.

What changed

  • "Tax Year" replaces "Previous Year" and "Assessment Year". Income earned from April 2026 to March 2027 is simply Tax Year 2026-27.
  • Sections and forms are renumbered. For example, the TDS certificate for salary — Form 16 — becomes Form 130. Familiar section numbers like 80C have new numbers too.
  • The new tax regime remains the default. You still need to choose the old regime actively if it suits you better.

  • A rebate of up to ₹60,000 means taxable income up to ₹12 lakh is effectively tax-free in the new regime. With the ₹75,000 standard deduction, salaried people pay no tax up to ₹12.75 lakh of salary. The rebate generally does not cover tax on special-rate income such as certain capital gains.
  • The old regime keeps its slabs (nil up to ₹2.5 lakh, 5%, 20% and 30%) and its deductions such as 80C, 80D and HRA, with a ₹50,000 standard deduction for salaried people.
  • A 4% health and education cess applies under both regimes.

Which law applies to the return you file next?

Returns for income earned up to 31 March 2026 (FY 2025-26) are still governed by the 1961 Act. The new Act applies to income from Tax Year 2026-27 onwards — which is what your employer's TDS and your advance tax this year are based on.
